# Preliminary critical-alert evidence review

This is an AI-assisted review of publicly available evidence, not validation by
an IDA financial-data specialist. Its purpose is to test whether alerts can be
interpreted responsibly and to identify where public evidence is insufficient.

| Country | Record | Preliminary outcome | Confidence | Evidence-based interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mauritania | FY20 commitments, USD 293M | Needs more information | Low | Multiple IDA approvals were identified, but the reviewed releases do not reconstruct the complete annual total. |
| Tanzania | FY20 commitments, USD 950M | Legitimate exception | High | The World Bank FY2020 annual report independently reports Tanzania at USD 950M, matching the source record. |
| Liberia | FY15 commitments, USD 257M | Needs more information | Low | Ebola-response and development operations explain exceptional activity, but the complete annual total was not reconstructed. |
| Uzbekistan | FY19 gross disbursements, USD 700M | Needs more information | Low | Public records show substantial reform and project activity, but the reviewed evidence does not establish the driver of the IDA disbursement total. |
| Nepal | FY17 commitments, USD 640M | Needs more information | Medium | Multiple large IDA credits were identified, but they do not yet provide a complete annual reconciliation. |
| Somalia | FY20 commitments, USD 902M | Legitimate exception | High | The FY2020 annual report lists approximately USD 903M, and Somalia restored access to IDA resources after clearing arrears in March 2020. |
| Nigeria | FY20 commitments, USD 2.576B | Legitimate exception | High | The FY2020 annual report lists the exact same amount and identifies Nigeria as the largest IDA country borrower that year. |
| Ukraine | FY23 gross disbursements, USD 984M | Legitimate exception | High | Public reporting documents extraordinary emergency financing and disbursement following the 2022 invasion. |

## Interpretation

Several critical alerts identify real, exceptional financing events rather than
erroneous records. This does not make them false positives: they were financially
unusual, material, and worthy of review. It also does not prove every critical
alert is valid; four cases remain unresolved because the public evidence reviewed
did not fully reconstruct the annual value or its underlying drivers.

The FY20 alerts belong to the IDA18 fiscal period. IDA19 began on July 1, 2020
and covered FY21–FY23. Therefore, FY20 movements must not be attributed to IDA19.
